\part{The Observation Boundary}
\label{part:observation}
% ============================================================

\noindent
Every physical measurement takes place within a structured epistemic boundary.
Before thermodynamic quantities can be assigned meaning, the maximum categorical
complexity accessible to any observer must be established.
Chapter~\ref{ch:obs} derives this boundary from the Bounded Phase Space Law
and shows that every thermodynamic quantity is an observable constructed within
it, not a pre-existing property of matter.

\chapter{Properties of the Observation Boundary}
\label{ch:obs}
\input{sections/p01_observation_boundary}

% ============================================================
\part{Dissolution of the Classical Paradoxes}
\label{part:paradoxes}
% ============================================================

\noindent
Three paradoxes have resisted thermodynamics for 150~years.
All three dissolve through the same conceptual move: replacing kinetic sorting
with categorical completion as the driver of thermodynamic evolution.
Gibbs's paradox, Maxwell's Demon, and Kelvin's heat death are addressed
in order of increasing scope~--- from mixing, to measurement, to the fate
of the universe.

\part{The Categorical Thermodynamic Framework}
\label{part:framework}
% ============================================================

\noindent
With the paradoxes cleared, the framework is built from first principles.
State counting, the Second Law, and behaviour at extreme low temperatures
are each derived as consequences of partition structure, not assumed as postulates.
The Third Law, in its usual form, constrains physical cooling; categorical
cooling operates in a commuting sector and is not constrained by it.

% ============================================================
\part{Equations of State: All Regimes}
\label{part:eos}
% ============================================================

\noindent
The categorical framework unifies all five canonical thermodynamic regimes~---
ideal gas, plasma, degenerate matter, relativistic gas, and Bose--Einstein
condensate~--- under a single structural factor $\mathcal{S}(V,N,\{n_i,\ell_i,m_i,s_i\})$.
These are not five separate theories.
They are five limiting expressions of one underlying partition geometry.
A single ion traversing a mass spectrometer demonstrates all five regimes
in sequence; the instrument provides the experimental thread for both chapters.

\part{Classical Thermodynamics as a Special Case}
\label{part:classical}
% ============================================================

\noindent
Classical kinetic theory emerges from the categorical framework as the
specialisation to sparse partition networks in thermal equilibrium.
The ideal gas law, Maxwell--Boltzmann statistics, and equipartition
are derived from partition statistics rather than assumed from kinetic
postulates.
The final chapter of this part establishes the Poincar\'e duality:
computation and thermodynamics are two descriptions of the same
partition traversal process.

\part{The Measurement Instrument}
\label{part:instrument}
% ============================================================

\noindent
Categorical measurement is not extraction of a pre-existing property.
It is synthesis through trajectory completion.
The mass spectrometer~--- four hardware oscillators forming a complete
set of commuting observables on partition coordinate space~--- resolves
$(n,\ell,m,s)$ directly from interference geometry.
The ion is measured into existence; it is not retrieved from a database.
