\section*{One Observation}

This monograph makes exactly one foundational claim, stated here as an axiom. Everything else — the Second Law, the equations of state, viscosity, the arrow of time, the ideal gas law, and the structure of fluid motion — is either a deductive consequence of this claim or an empirical confirmation of it.

\begin{axiom}[Bounded Phase Space Law]
\label{ax:bpsl}
Every \emph{persistent} dynamical system observed in Nature occupies a bounded region of phase space of finite Liouville measure, and that bounded region admits a nested hierarchy of measurable partitions into distinguishable sub-regions.
\end{axiom}

The word \emph{persistent} means: observed to maintain its identity over a macroscopic fraction of its natural dynamical timescale. An atom in a discharge lamp is persistent over its radiative lifetime. A gas molecule in a container is persistent over the container's confinement time. A star in a galaxy is persistent over its orbital period. The Axiom is empirical: no persistent physical system has ever been observed to occupy an unbounded region of phase space. The Axiom is defended by the whole of observational physics. To refute it, one must exhibit a persistent system that violates boundedness. No such system is known.

\medskip

From the Axiom, the following consequences follow by proof:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Poincaré recurrence.} Every trajectory in a bounded measure-preserving system returns, with probability one, to every neighbourhood of its starting point.
  \item \textbf{No persistence without bounds.} An ergodic system with infinite Liouville measure spends zero fraction of time in any bounded region. Persistence and boundedness are equivalent at the measure-theoretic level.
  \item \textbf{Oscillatory necessity.} Static, monotonic, and dispersive dynamics are each inconsistent with persistence on a bounded domain. Oscillatory dynamics is the unique self-consistent mode.
  \item \textbf{Discrete mode spectrum.} The Koopman operator of any persistent bounded dynamics has pure point spectrum. Every observable is a countable sum of periodic modes.
  \item \textbf{Energy--frequency identity.} $E = \hbar\omega$. The proportionality constant $\hbar$ is fixed by Bohr--Sommerfeld quantisation on bounded loops.
  \item \textbf{Partition coordinates.} Hierarchical refinement under $SO(3)$ symmetry generates the labelling $(n,\ell,m,s)$ with $\ell < n$, $|m| \leq \ell$, $s \in \{-\tfrac{1}{2}, +\tfrac{1}{2}\}$, and shell capacity $C(n) = 2n^{2}$.
  \item \textbf{Triple equivalence.} Oscillatory dynamics, categorical state enumeration, and partition operations are three coordinatisations of one process. The identity $dM/dt = \omega/(2\pi) = 1/\langle\tau_p\rangle$ expresses this.
  \item \textbf{Categorical entropy.} $S = k_{\mathrm{B}} M \ln n$. Entropy counts the categorical complexity of the partition traversal history.
  \item \textbf{Thermodynamics.} Temperature, pressure, the ideal gas law, Maxwell--Boltzmann statistics, the universal transport formula, and all five equations of state follow as consequences of the partition structure.
  \item \textbf{The Second Law.} $\Delta S > 0$ is a theorem, not a postulate. It follows from the asymmetry of categorical counting: forward traversal distributes probability over an expanding partition tree; exact time-reversal requires retracing the unique prior path, which is exponentially suppressed.
\end{enumerate}

This monograph covers consequences (vii) through (x): the triple equivalence, categorical entropy, thermodynamics in all its regimes, and the Second Law. The earlier consequences — atomic structure, special relativity, charge, nuclear physics — are treated in the companion volume \emph{On the Universal Consequences of Bounded Phase Space}.

\section*{On the Title}

\emph{Quid Est Quod Fuit} is taken from Ecclesiastes 1:9: \emph{``What is that which was? The same that shall be.''} In the context of this monograph it carries a precise meaning: what has been done is a categorical fact. Once a partition is completed, the non-actualisations it created — all the states that were not occupied — become permanent features of categorical space. They cannot be un-created. This is the deepest explanation of irreversibility: not that reversed trajectories are improbable, but that the non-actualisations they would require erasing are ontological facts, not physical configurations.

\emph{Sagitta Numquam Cadens} — the Arrow Never Falls — is the resolution of Zeno's arrow paradox. Zeno argued that an arrow in flight is at rest at each instant, and a sum of rests is not motion. The resolution is that motion is not a property of instants but of partition traversal: the arrow moves by completing categorical transitions, not by occupying a sequence of static positions. In thermodynamics, this is the statement that time itself is not a container in which processes occur but a count of completed partition operations. The identity $t = M/f$ — time equals partition count divided by oscillator frequency — is not a definition of convenience but the physical constitution of time.

Together the two phrases name the two faces of the Second Law: the past accumulates irrevocably (\emph{Quid Est Quod Fuit}), and its accumulation is what motion is (\emph{Sagitta Numquam Cadens}).

\section*{Reader's Map}

The monograph proceeds in seven parts.

\textbf{Part~I} establishes the epistemic boundary: how much can any observer know? The answer, $N_{\max} \approx (10^{84})\uparrow\uparrow(10^{80})$, is so large that every observer necessarily works within $\infty - x$, where $x$ is information structurally inaccessible to them. This is not an instrumental limitation; it is the geometry of observation. Every thermodynamic quantity is a construct within this boundary.

\textbf{Part~II} dissolves the three paradoxes that have resisted thermodynamics for 150 years. Gibbs's paradox, Maxwell's Demon, and Kelvin's heat death all rest on the same error: they assume thermodynamic evolution is driven by kinetic sorting. It is driven by categorical completion. Once this is seen, all three paradoxes dissolve without statistical argument, quantum indistinguishability, or information-theoretic cost.

\textbf{Part~III} builds the framework: categorical state counting as the foundation of thermodynamics. The Second Law, the arrow of time, and the heat--entropy decoupling theorem are each derived as consequences of partition structure. The extreme low-temperature limit — categorical cryogenics — shows that the Third Law constrains physical cooling, not categorical cooling.

\textbf{Part~IV} applies the framework to all five thermodynamic regimes: neutral gas, plasma, degenerate matter, relativistic gas, and Bose--Einstein condensate. The universal equation of state $PV = Nk_{\mathrm{B}}T \cdot \mathcal{S}(V,N,\{n_i,\ell_i,m_i,s_i\})$ subsumes all five within a single structural factor. A single ion traversing a mass spectrometer passes through conditions that probe all five regimes.

\textbf{Part~V} recovers the classical results. The ideal gas law is proven from partition statistics, not assumed. Maxwell--Boltzmann emerges as optimal load distribution across partition modes. The Poincaré duality of computation and thermodynamics is established: a gas is a computer; a computer is a gas.

\textbf{Part~VI} presents the measurement instrument. Categorical measurement is not extraction of a pre-existing value but synthesis through trajectory completion. A mass spectrometer — four hardware oscillators forming a Complete Set of Commuting Observables on partition coordinate space — resolves $(n,\ell,m,s)$ directly. The molecule is measured into existence: 847 compounds, 1.1\,ppm mean error, 100\% inter-modality consistency.

\textbf{Part~VII} treats matter in motion: spectral-native gas and fluid dynamics. Particles are instantiated from hardware oscillations via S-entropy coordinates. Their interference computes observables. A GPU shader pipeline renders and measures simultaneously, making explicit the rendering--measurement identity: the ray march through the volume IS the thermodynamic observation.
